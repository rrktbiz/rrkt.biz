▶The Ryuo Clan Arc, Ep. 7

A quiet side road on the outskirts, away from the city center.
There stood a small, unremarkable building.

The inside of the shop was filled with all manner of dreary things.
Voodoo dolls, curse talismans with intricate patterns, a Japanese girl doll standing with a pale face. A curio shop packed with implements of sorcery of unknown origin.

The interior, relying on a single light, was dark and dim.

Jingle-.

With the clear ring of a bell, the shop door opened.
At the counter, a clerk in a black kimono calmly greeted the customer.

The customer who entered glanced around suspiciously as he approached the counter. In a low voice, he whispered a secret password.


***

"Was there something you needed?"

Meanwhile, in a shadowed alley of the city.
Aoi, expressionless, took a call from a client.

[ "Won't you take on an employer? Exclusively for our family..." ]

Aoi's eyes narrowed.

"Moushiwake nain desu ga, our house does not keep an exclusive employer."
[ "....~~" ]

The client's expression twisted.
He raised his voice one last time.

[ "If you receive a commission to kill me, I'll pay ten times that amount... no, as much as you want." ]

Aoi answered evenly, as though he were well used to this.

"We don't make that kind of deal. ....Do take care not to earn any grudges."

Click.

He hung up.


***

Meanwhile,
Kuro was touring the family's business sites with Jiji.

"This here is my grandson. The lad who'll be taking over as head, na no ja."

Kuro, awkward and hesitant with everything so unfamiliar, nonetheless paid close attention to what his grandfather did as head.

First they visited the Ryuo hot springs. Unlike the entrance, with its gaudy gilded dragon crest, the facilities grew rustier and shabbier the further in one went. There were more foreign tourists than local customers.

Next came the restaurants under the family's umbrella. The kitchen storeroom was packed with boxes of ingredients. On every box could be seen the logo of a distributor bearing the name of one relative or another.

The last stop was the factory that churned out the family's merchandise. There, amid a thick whitish smog, workers carried goods with expressionless faces. Cheap products were packed in wrapping paper stamped with the family's classy dragon crest. The factory manager held out the ledger to Jiji, bowing again and again.

Kuro felt something strangely off, but no one else seemed to pay it any mind. He set his doubts aside and let himself blend into the mood. He couldn't summon the courage to cut in among these impressive adults with a question, and he was afraid that if he spoke up needlessly, his grandfather would snap at him.

Kuro merely stood on the path the adults had paved, matching their pace as best he could read it.


***

Under a fine sky, the quiet entrance to a shrine.
Aoi stood in the shade of a nearby tree.

"Aoi-san!"

From afar, Kuro came running, beaming, in a burgundy kimono.

Aoi's eyes went round at the sight of Kuro in a kimono, and then a gentle smile spread across his face.

Terribly bashful under that gaze, Kuro posed so that the kimono he wore could be seen well.

"Hehe.. You looked so cool in your kimono, Aoi-san, so I matched my outfit to yours."

The pure confession of a boy who had gone so far as to dress to match him. As if finding it adorable, Aoi answered with an easy smile.

"Kawaii yan~"

**

With a spring in his step, Kuro led the way, guiding Aoi.

"Our town worships all sorts of gods da kedo... the really big one is.."

They stood at the entrance of a grand shrine, where a great torii gate stood.

".....the Ryuo Shrine."

Even as he explained, Kuro was a little embarrassed, as it felt like bragging about his own shrine.

Aoi replied, looking the shrine over.

"Ookii na~"


*

Clink.

Aoi tossed a coin into the offering box and finished his prayer.
On the front of the shrine, a huge dragon was painted.

"Hee, so it enshrines a dragon god, does it. Now that's unusual~.."
"Yeah, I'm told it's been handed down all the way from our ancestors."
"Hoh~"

Aoi skimmed a pamphlet provided at the shrine.
He could see that countless businesses—the Ryuo hot springs, restaurants, and so on—were intertwined, centered on the shrine.

"So the Ryuo family runs all sorts o' businesses 'round the shrine, huh~"
"That's right. The commercial facilities in this town—about half of them belong to the Ryuo family."
"Sugoi yan, the Ryuo family~"

Kuro spoke up cautiously.

"But.. we've been scaling back the businesses for a few years now. Fewer and fewer people come to the shrine, too.. My cousin says it's 'because the Ryuo family's way of running things is outdated'..."

Kuro wore a bittersweet expression.
Hearing that, Aoi looked around the Ryuo shrine once more.

There were none of the flashy photo spots or character charms of the shrines that were all the rage these days. A weathered signboard at the entrance and an offering box were all there was.

Standing there all alone, clinging solely to the family's traditions, without even an effort to catch the eye of tourists.

It was a shrine where time had stopped.

"Hmm.. the tide of the times, I s'pose. Must be a lot weighin' on ya as head."


***

"This one, an' this one, an' this one~ hmm~ I'll take these, please~"
"That many!?"

Kuro's eyes went round.

Aoi began sweeping up boxes of Ryuo-brand Japanese sweets and local specialties from the display stands.

"For my little brothers an' sisters."
"Hee— you must have a lot of siblings, Aoi-san."
"Mm-hm, fufu~ I got quite a lot of 'em~~ 'bout thirty thousand~"

Kuro stared at Aoi, dumbfounded.
Thirty thousand—it was an absurd number.

'Aoi-san, I can't tell how much of that is a joke...'


***

A bench near the shrine.

Aoi set down his mountain of purchases and sat resting side by side with Kuro.

"Hoh~ yappa, sure am glad I came with the family head-san~ A fine guide-san ya~"
"I'm glad I could help!"

Aoi paused before speaking.

"Bet I surprised ya, askin' ya outta the blue to show me 'round the shrine?"

At that, Kuro looked steadily at Aoi.

"Uchi no family's a sorcery family that works on short-term commissions. We travel a lot for work, movin' from place to place, so settlin' into an unfamiliar spot makes me uneasy, y'see. So whenever I come to a new town, I got in the habit o' payin' my respects to that town's god. Somethin' like, 'I'm an outsider, but please look kindly on me~' Fufu."
"Sokka..."

Kuro answered with a bright smile.

"I'm sure all the land gods of this town will welcome you and your family, Aoi-san!"

At that, Aoi thought for a moment, then offered a playful prayer toward Kuro.

"Haai. Please look kindly on me. Dragon God-sama."
"Eh? Ah!!"
"Hmm?"

When Kuro got flustered, Aoi cracked one eye open and looked at him.

"I'm that Dragon God!!"

Aoi burst out laughing.

"Pfhaha, ya didn't realize?"

To Aoi's question, Kuro answered with a rather serious air.

"Datte, I myself am a super ordinary person, so..."

The look in Aoi's eyes changed subtly.
Belatedly, Kuro hurried on as if to reassure Aoi.

"O-of course Lackness...no, the Dragon God will welcome you, Aoi-san!"
"Yokatta~"

Kuro hesitantly added.

"....I feel bad for the people who come to pray to the Dragon God, but the Dragon God is only strong in power, and as a god, how do I put it.. no matter what wish you pray for, he can't grant it..."

Kuro said it with a guilt-ridden expression.
Looking at him, Aoi glanced down at the pamphlet.
The Ryuo family's motto was written there.

[ May the Ryuo family be a comfort and a haven for the weary. ]

From hot springs and traditional inns built on the running of the shrine, to restaurants making use of local specialties. It was a motto befitting the Ryuo family's business structure.

With a look of resignation, Aoi spoke calmly.

"It's all right. Folks just wanna take a little comfort, even for a moment. Even if their wishes ain't granted, they won't blame the Dragon God for it, an'—"

It might depend on how one took it, but this was Aoi's own way of consoling him. Then Aoi shifted the mood and turned his head toward Kuro.

"If a Dragon God this cute an' kind is there to welcome 'em, that alone'd be comfort enough."

With an easy smile, Aoi offered a plausible consolation, as if soothing a small child.

"Th-thank you.."
"......"

But contrary to his thanks, Kuro wore an expression that said he wasn't convinced by Aoi's words.

After that, Kuro fell silent for a while, as if lost in his own thoughts.

Aoi gazed steadily at him.

"......"

Shhhh—.

A wind blew, scattering leaves along with a chill in the air.

"......Kuro-chan."

At Aoi's low voice, Kuro flinched and stiffened.

Striking a pose of prayer, Aoi looked at Kuro out of the corner of his eye.

And asked.

"Would the Dragon God welcome even a murderer—shite kureru?"

[End of The Ryuo Clan Arc, Ep. 7]
