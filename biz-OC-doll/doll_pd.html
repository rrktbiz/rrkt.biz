Creator's Note
Why it's made this way
The culture we grew up with

We used to love the Vocaloid fan culture. Even with only a few lines of character info, people spun stories out of it, others made something new from those stories, and it all spread out into all kinds of content. That was the fun of it. Beyond Vocaloid, too, there used to be a livelier culture than today of fan works, and works built on those, that stood on their own as real creations.

In the ten-odd years since, subculture has grown far bigger. Mobile games now sit at the center of it all, and a clear flow has settled in: the official side releases new characters and stories, fans take them in and enjoy them through art and cosplay, then go looking for them in merch and pop-up stores. It's an era where showing love through spending feels natural, and it's true that this has let many more people enjoy the culture. But by the same token, the sight of people freely setting each other's creativity alight from almost nothing has become hard to find. We don't think that's anyone's fault. It's a change that came naturally with the times. So rather than waiting for those days to return, this project is closer to a struggle to make even one place where that kind of scene could happen again.

That's why the character structure of this project was designed the way it is below. Even if the story ends, we hope these characters never do.

What fan culture taught us

Watching fan culture for a long time taught us that the less the official side says, the more fans fill in. So the character sheets are made to be closer to raw material than a finished product. If you're used to receiving a finished story and enjoying it, this approach may feel unfamiliar at first, since you have to fill in the spaces the official side leaves open yourself.

Like any creator, when we first imagined this project, it wasn't easy to let the characters go. Then, as we shifted to a structure where one character branches out differently with each Holder, we were able to loosen our grip a little. Now fans are welcome to interpret the characters freely, and it's fine if their take becomes more famous than what was first imagined. That's what this project was built hoping for in the first place. Someday the official side may also name a character and create with it, standing in the same place as every other Holder. Even then, the name and personality will change from work to work, so the official version will be just one of that code's many faces, never the right answer.

Never ending it

This project has no finale and no finished state. Not ending is its baseline. Taking Cinderella Girls as a reference, we set the goal at 200 characters or more, but it's hard to promise how far it will go. Not everything always goes to plan. What we promise instead is not to stop.

The release order, too, follows whichever character we feel like making at the time rather than some complex strategy. Keeping the character sheets rough and the workload light is for the same reason. That's how it can keep going for a long time.

Branding / one color

biz-OC-doll is filled only with characters the official side genuinely loves. They're made to the official side's taste, and that direction isn't going to change. Just as a good label has a color you can recognize at a glance, we believe this project needs a color of its own too. If that color started to waver, the solidity holding this project up would fade along with it. The color gives the official side strength as well. It feels like growing a collection of favorite things one piece at a time, and that's what makes it possible to stay with this project for a long time.

Because of that, there will surely be people who wish their favorite type of character were here. If those people start open character projects of their own, we'd be truly glad. Characters with that place's own color would come out of it. We wouldn't see that as following us or copying us. If anything, many projects like this springing up and filling the scene with their own colors is closer to the picture we described earlier.

The scene we want to see

The scene we most want to see from this project is biz-OC-dolls appearing in all kinds of works, like actors. A character who's the lead in one artist's webcomic shows up as a villain in someone else's game, and as a supporting role who passes by for a moment in yet another web novel. The name and personality differ in every work, but follow them back to the original code and it's the same face, and all those appearances add up to that character's filmography. If that happens a lot, we think it will be the most moving scene this project can show.

Part of what led to this structure is a regret the official side has carried for a long time. Whenever a work with a beloved character reached its end, it always felt sad, as if the character had ended along with it. We wanted to keep loving them, but with nothing new coming out, the affection would cool on its own, and it felt like the character was trapped inside the work. We wanted to keep seeing them by giving them new stories, at least in our own original works, but of course we couldn't use someone else's character as we pleased. In the end we let them go with a bitter feeling, and after that happened again and again, it became hard to find the courage to love a new character. Wanting a way out of that dilemma is what became this structure.

For a fandom to never end, someone has to keep making things, and for that, anyone has to be able to come back easily. Every otaku has that one fandom they can always go back to, don't they? Not blazing brightly, but quietly staying alive, with people still there whenever you return. We hope biz-OC-doll becomes that kind of fandom, a place where scattered tastes gather and anyone can rekindle the fire at any time. Here, going beyond fan works, simply taking a character into your own work makes you a creator who moves the whole scene. Instead of just watching a beloved work come to an end, you can keep the scene you love going yourself. Staying around for a long time as a fandom built together like that is what this project hopes to be.

So you can create with peace of mind

A few years ago, while this project was still taking shape, we casually suggested to a creator we're close with that they make something using a biz-OC-doll character. They happily agreed, but as time went on, we could see them growing uneasy. They asked whether using someone else's character might cause legal trouble later, and whether they should sign some kind of contract. This was even though we were simply happy to see the character used and had no intention of asking for anything.

That's when we learned that, for the person using the character, this can bring up quite complicated feelings. Love alone isn't enough; people need to be able to trust before they can create with peace of mind. It was then that we took to heart that this project can only work if the trust between the official side and Holders is solid.

So as long as your work follows the published rules, there's no need to come ask for permission. Those rules are the official side's permission.

The atmosphere we protect

biz-OC-doll Official aims to protect its own atmosphere, clear and sparkling like a gem, just like the feel of this site you're looking at now. We think it's the makers' job to keep everything, from the characters to how things are run, from straying out of that atmosphere.

To put your mind at ease up front: the official side will never be the one shaking up the scene. Looking at how this project is built, some of you may worry that it's all a way to hold the scene in the palm of our hand and play with it. We know the kind of scars that sort of management leaves on a community, so we won't do it. We won't throw out provocative bait or controversies to see how people react, and we won't test anything on the fandom. In other words, there will never be a day when we say, "Actually, this was a social experiment."

In the end, we believe a scene's atmosphere comes to resemble what the official side steadily puts out. So the surest thing the official side can do is hold the center. If the official side wavers, the fandom can't play at ease. That's why we're writing down here what the official side intends to protect, and then focusing on releasing characters.

Even with a worldview

biz-OC-doll has an official worldview ready. It hasn't been revealed yet, but the foundation of the world the characters live in is already decided. That said, hearing that there's a separate official worldview might actually feel like pressure. When official settings exist, you may feel self-conscious about taking your biz-OC-doll your own way, and start to wonder whether you're wrong if it differs from the official version.

So the very first rule we set when building the worldview was that it must not clash with what happens in this project. We can't go into detail yet, but in that world too, biz-OC-dolls share the same face based on their code while each living with a different way of talking and a different story. Choosing a biz-OC-doll, naming it, and raising it in your own way were all made to carry over naturally into that world as well.

So it's fine not to know the worldview, and it's fine to create stories completely different from it. The official worldview isn't a frame that locks in Holders' biz-OC-dolls. It's built as a world where that kind of creativity is welcome in any amount. If we get to show it someday as a demo game, you'll be able to feel better what we meant by this.

Official merch

Some of you may be waiting for the official side to release some merch, especially those who love the characters as their codes are, before any name is given. However, biz-OC-doll Official has no plans to make merch directly for now. We think official merch is more likely to come out through collaborations with companies. When a collaboration happens, many kinds of merch often come out all at once, so we hope you'll wait just a little for that time.

In the meantime, we'd love for merch made by Holders to fill that space. We hope a culture takes root in this fandom where merch made by Holders is treasured just as much as official merch. If unofficial goods fill the official place until official ones arrive, we think that too is very much in the spirit of this project.

Collaboration
If we build it together

biz-OC-doll was designed from the start as an IP that can collaborate with others. For a dream like this to last, it needs a design that can hold it up, so biz-OC-doll Official built the structure first. At its center are the number of characters and the official worldview.

Number of characters

The goal of 200 characters is set high so that, even if each character's fandom differs in size, together they form an IP at a scale worth considering for collaboration. Because every character is made under the same standards and atmosphere, they read as a single IP no matter how many there are.

Official worldview

Building a worldview only after a collaboration is decided tends to mean racing the clock, and the IP's character can shift before and after the collaboration, shaking the trust fans have built up. So biz-OC-doll Official aligned the project's character, the way it is run, and its worldview to point in the same direction from the start. This keeps the foundation firm whatever compromises a collaboration may involve, and because fan creations rest on the same structure, there is little risk of a collaboration clashing with the existing fandom.

What has been set in advance is the worldview; the stories built on it are left open to be shaped together in a collaboration. The worldview will be shown through a demo game, planned for release once there are around 50 or more characters.

Turning this worldview into a full-fledged work will only come after the fandom has grown enough, which could take as long as ten years, and we also keep in mind that it may never happen. Even so, the demo game is being made ahead of time. Once released, the demo game and worldview notes will be collected below.

Open to collaboration

The official worldview is somewhat removed from what the market currently favors and is fairly niche. For that reason, the official worldview is not a condition for collaboration. If you've come for the character IP, starting lightly with a new story separate from the official worldview may actually be the better fit. Short-running games, experimental projects, and small games or merch that borrow the characters for a while are all welcome. Every biz-OC-doll collaboration becomes part of the IP's track record and a chance for the partner to show off its technology, so we believe it becomes a meaningful record for both sides.

In this way, biz-OC-doll is structured so that the whole IP never rides on the outcome of a single work. A new story made through a collaboration is still unmistakably biz-OC-doll, while the original and the official worldview remain separate from it. So there's no need to feel much pressure about trying something new.

It isn't something for right now, but we'd like to note the direction in advance. biz-OC-doll is being built to be an IP that keeps pace with advancing technology and can showcase it. We believe that the further technology advances, the more vividly characters come to life and the more closely they can reach more people. If you have that kind of technology, we're glad to work together, starting with small experiments, whatever your size.

Collaboration principles

These principles do not change in any collaboration.

  1. 1The original copyright belongs to biz-OC-doll and is not transferred in any form.
  2. 2Companies may use the characters only within the permitted scope.
  3. 3Rights to fan creations belong to their creators. A company wishing to use them must reach an individual agreement with the creator.
  4. 4Companies may not call themselves official or represent biz-OC-doll.
  5. 5Companies take no part in the production or operation of the project.
  6. 6Individual custom requests are not accepted.
All specific terms and scope of a collaboration are set by individual contract.
Even before its public release, the demo game can be shared with you first as soon as it's ready, on request.
Rights
Rights

We have seen, more than once, creators who failed to sort out their rights properly and later could not freely use their own characters even in their own works. So the official side makes it a rule to complete copyright registration before releasing a character. The purpose is not to hold on to particular rights or to police anyone.
